Lesson

To measure the area under a curve $y=f(x)$ on $[a,b]$, approximate it with rectangles, then let the number of rectangles grow without bound.

Riemann sums

Split $[a,b]$ into $n$ equal subintervals of width $\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}$, with endpoints $x_0=a, x_1, \dots, x_n=b$. Pick a sample point $x_i^*$ in each subinterval $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$ and form

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x$$

the sum of the rectangles' signed areas. Common choices for $x_i^*$: the left endpoint $x_{i-1}$, the right endpoint $x_i$, or the midpoint $\frac{x_{i-1}+x_i}{2}$.

Definition — the definite integral

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x$$

whenever this limit exists (it does for any continuous $f$), regardless of which sample points $x_i^*$ were used. The definite integral is that limit — a Riemann sum is just a finite approximation to it.

Example

Approximate $\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^2\,dx$ using a right Riemann sum with $n=4$.

$\Delta x = \frac{2-0}{4} = 0.5$, so the subintervals are $[0,0.5],[0.5,1],[1,1.5],[1.5,2]$, and the right endpoints are $0.5, 1, 1.5, 2$.

$$\sum_{i=1}^{4} f(x_i)\,\Delta x = 0.5\big[(0.5)^2+(1)^2+(1.5)^2+(2)^2\big] = 0.5\,[0.25+1+2.25+4] = 0.5(7.5) = 3.75$$

(The exact value, found in the next few lessons, is $\frac{8}{3}\approx 2.67$ — a right sum overestimates here since $x^2$ is increasing.)

Practice Examples

Work out your answers to the following questions on paper, then check your answers below.

  1. 1 Let $f(x)=x^2$. Use a right Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{2} f(x)\,dx$.
  2. 2 Let $f(x)=x^2$. Use a left Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{2} f(x)\,dx$.
  3. 3 Let $f(x)=4-x^2$. Use a right Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{2} f(x)\,dx$.
  4. 4 Let $f(x)=x+1$. Use a midpoint Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{1}^{5} f(x)\,dx$.
  5. 5 Let $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$. Use a right Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{4} f(x)\,dx$.
  6. 6 Let $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}$. Use a left Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{1}^{3} f(x)\,dx$.
  7. 7 Let $f(x)=x^3$. Use a right Riemann sum with $n=5$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{1} f(x)\,dx$.
  8. 8 Let $f(x)=9-x^2$. Use a midpoint Riemann sum with $n=3$ to approximate $\int_{-1}^{2} f(x)\,dx$.
  9. 9 Let $f(x)=\cos(x)$. Use a left Riemann sum with $n=6$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{\pi/2} f(x)\,dx$.
  10. 10 Let $f(x)=e^{-x}$. Use a right Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{2} f(x)\,dx$.
  11. 11 Without computing it, decide whether a right Riemann sum for $\int_0^2 (4-x^2)\,dx$ is an overestimate or an underestimate of the true area, and explain why in one sentence.
  12. 12 Without computing it, decide whether a left Riemann sum for $\int_1^3 \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$ is an overestimate or an underestimate, and explain why in one sentence.
  13. 13 For $\displaystyle\int_2^6 (x^2+1)\,dx$, write out $\Delta x$ and the general right-endpoint $x_i$ used to build the Riemann sum $\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i)\Delta x$ for arbitrary $n$. (Do not evaluate the sum.)
  14. 14 Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2}{n}\left(1+\frac{2i}{n}\right)^2$ by first recognizing it as a definite integral.
  15. 15 Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{3}{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{3i}{n}\right)$ by first recognizing it as a definite integral.
  16. 16 Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^3 \sqrt{9-x^2}\,dx$ by interpreting it as the area of a familiar geometric region.
  17. 17 Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^4 (2x+3)\,dx$ by interpreting it as the area of a trapezoid, without antidifferentiating.
  18. 18 Let $f(x)=x^2$. Use a right Riemann sum with $n=6$ to approximate $\int_{0}^{3} f(x)\,dx$.
  19. 19 Let $f(x)=\ln(x)$. Use a midpoint Riemann sum with $n=4$ to approximate $\int_{1}^{3} f(x)\,dx$.
  20. 20 For $f(x)=x^2$ on $[0,3]$, write the general right Riemann sum $R_n=\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i)\Delta x$ in closed form as a function of $n$, then take $\lim_{n\to\infty}R_n$ to find the exact value of $\int_0^3 x^2\,dx$.
