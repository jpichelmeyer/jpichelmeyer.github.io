Lesson

So far, every series has had fixed numerical terms. A power series instead has terms that depend on $x$, so it defines a function — wherever it happens to converge.

Definition

A power series centered at $a$ is $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} c_i (x-a)^i$. Every power series converges at $x=a$ itself (every term but the first vanishes); the question is for which other $x$ it converges.

Radius and interval of convergence

For every power series, exactly one of these holds:

• it converges only at $x=a$ (radius $R=0$);

• it converges for all $x$ (radius $R=\infty$);

• it converges for $|x-a|R$, for some $0

Find $R$ with the Ratio Test applied to the terms $c_i(x-a)^i$, treating $x$ as fixed; then test each endpoint on its own, since the Ratio Test itself is inconclusive there.

Example

Find the interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-2)^i}{i\,3^i}$.

Ratio Test: $$\left|\frac{a_{i+1}}{a_i}\right| = \left|\frac{(x-2)^{i+1}}{(i+1)3^{i+1}}\cdot\frac{i\,3^i}{(x-2)^i}\right| = \frac{i}{i+1}\cdot\frac{|x-2|}{3} \to \frac{|x-2|}{3}$$

Convergence needs $\frac{|x-2|}{3}<1$, i.e. $|x-2|<3$, so $R=3$ and the series converges on $(-1,5)$ before checking endpoints.

At $x=5$: the series becomes $\sum \frac{3^i}{i\,3^i}=\sum\frac1i$, the harmonic series — diverges.

At $x=-1$: the series becomes $\sum \frac{(-3)^i}{i\,3^i}=\sum\frac{(-1)^i}{i}$, the alternating harmonic series — converges (conditionally).

So the full interval of convergence is $[-1, 5)$.

Practice Examples

Work out your answers to the following questions on paper, then check your answers below.

  1. 1 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^i}{i!}$.
  2. 2 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-2)^i}{i}$.
  3. 3 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} i\,x^i$.
  4. 4 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(x+1)^i}{3^i}$.
  5. 5 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^i x^{2i}}{(2i)!}$.
  6. 6 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^i}{i^2}$.
  7. 7 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{(3x)^i}{i!}$.
  8. 8 Find the radius and interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-1)^i}{i\,2^i}$.

  10. 10 Represent $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}$ as a power series centered at $0$, and give its radius of convergence.
  11. 11 Represent $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{1-x^3}$ as a power series centered at $0$, and give its radius of convergence.
  12. 12 Represent $f(x)=\dfrac{3}{2-x}$ as a power series centered at $0$, and give its radius of convergence.
  13. 13 Set up (do not fully expand) how you would represent $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{4+x^2}$ as a power series centered at $0$, by first factoring out a constant to match the form $\dfrac{1}{1-u}$.

  17. 17 Explain why a power series' interval of convergence is always an interval centered at $a$ (for a series in powers of $x-a$) — never, say, a union of two disjoint intervals.
  18. 18 List the three possible types of intervals of convergence a power series can have.
  19. 19 Use the Ratio Test to explain why $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^i}{i!}$ (the series for $e^x$) has an infinite radius of convergence.
  20. 20 If a power series $\sum c_i(x-3)^i$ converges at $x=8$, what can you conclude about its behavior at $x=1$?
