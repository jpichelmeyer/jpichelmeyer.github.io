Lesson

A curve's length is approximated by chopping it into short straight segments and adding up their lengths — the Pythagorean Theorem applied to each tiny piece, then a limit turns the sum into an integral.

Arc length formula

If $f'$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, the length of $y=f(x)$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is

$$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}\,dx$$

The $1$ under the root comes from a horizontal step of length $dx$; $[f'(x)]^2$ accounts for the corresponding vertical rise, since $\frac{dy}{dx}=f'(x)$ means the rise over that same step is approximately $f'(x)\,dx$.

Example

Find the length of $y = \dfrac{2}{3}x^{3/2}$ from $x=0$ to $x=3$.

$f'(x) = x^{1/2}$, so $[f'(x)]^2 = x$.

$$L = \int_0^3 \sqrt{1+x}\,dx = \left[\frac23(1+x)^{3/2}\right]_0^3 = \frac23\big(4^{3/2}-1^{3/2}\big) = \frac23(8-1) = \frac{14}{3}$$
