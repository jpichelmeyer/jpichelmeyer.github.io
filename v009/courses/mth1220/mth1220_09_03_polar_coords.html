Lesson

Polar coordinates locate a point by distance and direction from the origin instead of horizontal/vertical displacement — a natural fit for curves with circular or spiral symmetry that are awkward to describe with $y=f(x)$.

Definition and conversion

A point's polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ mean: distance $r$ from the origin, at angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis. Conversion to and from rectangular coordinates:

$$x = r\cos\theta, \qquad y=r\sin\theta, \qquad r^2=x^2+y^2, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}$$

$r$ is allowed to be negative (plot in the opposite direction), and $(r,\theta)$ isn't unique — the same point has infinitely many representations, e.g. $(r,\theta)=(r,\theta+2\pi)$.

Common polar curves

$r=a$: a circle of radius $a$ centered at the origin. $\ \ \theta=b$: a line through the origin at angle $b$.

$r=a(1+\cos\theta)$ or $r=a(1+\sin\theta)$: a cardioid (heart-shaped). $\ \ r=a\cos(n\theta)$ or $r=a\sin(n\theta)$: a rose with $n$ petals if $n$ is odd, $2n$ petals if $n$ is even.

Example

Convert the polar point $\left(4, \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$ to rectangular coordinates, and identify the curve $r=6\cos\theta$.

$x = 4\cos\frac\pi3 = 4\cdot\frac12=2$, $\ y=4\sin\frac\pi3 = 4\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=2\sqrt3$, so the point is $(2, 2\sqrt3)$.

For the curve: multiply both sides by $r$: $r^2=6r\cos\theta$, i.e. $x^2+y^2=6x$. Complete the square: $(x-3)^2+y^2=9$ — a circle of radius $3$ centered at $(3,0)$.

Practice Examples

Work out your answers to the following questions on paper, then check your answers below.

  1. 1 Convert the point $\left(4,\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$ (polar) to rectangular coordinates.
  2. 2 Convert the point $\left(2,\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)$ (polar) to rectangular coordinates.
  3. 3 Convert the point $(1,1)$ (rectangular) to polar coordinates with $r>0$ and $0\le\theta<2\pi$.
  4. 4 Convert the point $(-\sqrt{3},1)$ (rectangular) to polar coordinates with $r>0$ and $0\le\theta<2\pi$.
  5. 5 Convert $x^2+y^2=9$ to a polar equation.
  6. 6 Convert $x=5$ to a polar equation.
  7. 7 Convert $y=x$ (with $x>0$) to a polar equation.
  8. 8 Convert $x^2+y^2=2x$ to a polar equation.
  9. 9 Convert the polar equation $r=\dfrac{2}{1-\cos\theta}$ to a rectangular equation, and identify the conic.
  10. 10 Classify the curve $r=3+3\cos\theta$, and state how many petals or loops it has (if applicable).
  11. 11 Classify the curve $r=1+2\cos\theta$. Does it have an inner loop?
  12. 12 Classify the curve $r=\cos(3\theta)$, and state how many petals it has.
  13. 13 Classify the curve $r=\sin(4\theta)$, and state how many petals it has.
  14. 14 Classify the curve $r^2=4\cos(2\theta)$.
  15. 15 Use a symmetry test to explain why $r=2+2\sin\theta$ is symmetric about the line $\theta=\pi/2$ (the $y$-axis).
  16. 16 Use a symmetry test to explain why $r=3\cos\theta$ is symmetric about the polar axis (the $x$-axis).
  17. 17 What interval of $\theta$ traces the full rose $r=\cos(3\theta)$ exactly once (with no repeated points)?
  18. 18 Describe the shape of the curve $r=\theta$ for $\theta\ge 0$.
  19. 19 For the limaçon $r=a+b\cos\theta$, what condition on $a$ and $b$ (with $a,b>0$) produces an inner loop, versus a dimpled limaçon with no loop?
  20. 20 Explain why the point $(2,\pi)$ and the point $(-2,0)$ in polar coordinates represent the same location in the plane.

Answers

  1. 1.$(2,\ 2\sqrt{3})$
  2. 2.$(-1,\ \sqrt{3})$
  3. 3.$\left(\sqrt{2},\ \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$
  4. 4.$\left(2,\ \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$
  5. 5.$r=3$
  6. 6.$r=5\sec\theta$
  7. 7.$\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
  8. 8.$r=2\cos\theta$
  9. 9.$y^2=4x+4$ — a parabola.
  10. 10.A cardioid (heart-shaped, one loop, no inner loop since the constant term equals the coefficient).
  11. 11.A limaçon with an inner loop, since $|{-1}| \cdots$ the constant ($1$) is less than the coefficient ($2$).
  12. 12.A rose curve with $3$ petals (odd $n$ in $r=\cos(n\theta)$ gives $n$ petals).
  13. 13.A rose curve with $8$ petals (even $n$ in $r=\sin(n\theta)$ gives $2n$ petals).
  14. 14.A lemniscate (figure-eight shape).
  15. 15.Replacing $\theta$ with $\pi-\theta$ leaves the equation unchanged, since $\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta$ — this is the symmetry test for the vertical axis.
  16. 16.Replacing $\theta$ with $-\theta$ leaves the equation unchanged, since $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$.
  17. 17.$\theta\in[0,\pi)$
  18. 18.An Archimedean spiral: it winds outward from the origin, moving farther from the pole by a constant amount for each full turn.
  19. 19.An inner loop occurs when $ab$; when $a\ge 2b$ the curve is a smooth convex limaçon.
  20. 20.A negative radius $r$ plots the point in the opposite direction: $(-2,0)$ points along $\theta=0$ but backward, landing at the same spot as $(2,\pi)$, which points along $\theta=\pi$ forward.
