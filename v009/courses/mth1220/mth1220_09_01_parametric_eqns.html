Lesson

Not every curve is the graph of a function $y=f(x)$ — a circle fails the vertical line test, for one. Parametric equations describe a curve by giving both $x$ and $y$ as functions of a third variable, a parameter $t$, which usually stands in for time.

Definition

A parametric curve is given by $x=x(t)$, $y=y(t)$ for $t$ in some interval. As $t$ increases, the point $(x(t),y(t))$ traces the curve out in a specific direction.

Slope of a parametric curve

By the Chain Rule, $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = \dfrac{dy}{dx}\cdot\dfrac{dx}{dt}$, which rearranges to

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} \qquad \left(\frac{dx}{dt}\ne 0\right)$$

Notice this never requires solving for $y$ as a function of $x$ at all.

Example 1 — eliminating the parameter

Identify the curve $x=\cos t$, $y=\sin t$, $0\le t\le 2\pi$.

Since $x^2+y^2=\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1$, every point lies on the unit circle. As $t$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, the point traces the full circle once, counterclockwise, starting at $(1,0)$.

Example 2 — slope without eliminating $t$

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x=t^2$, $y=t^3-3t$ at $t=2$.

$\dfrac{dx}{dt}=2t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=3t^2-3$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{3t^2-3}{2t}$. At $t=2$: $\dfrac{3(4)-3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.
