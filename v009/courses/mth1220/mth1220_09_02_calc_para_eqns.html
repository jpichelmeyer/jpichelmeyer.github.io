Practice Examples

Work out your answers to the following questions on paper, then check your answers below.

  1. 1 Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t=1$ for $x=t^2,\ y=t^3$.
  2. 2 Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t=\pi/4$ for $x=\cos t,\ y=\sin t$.
  3. 3 Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t=2$ for $x=t^3-3t,\ y=t^2$.
  4. 4 Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ (in terms of $t$) for $x=t^2+1,\ y=t^3+t$, and use it to say where the curve is concave up.
  5. 5 Find the area under $x=t^2,\ y=t$ for $t\in[0,2]$ (i.e. between the curve and the $x$-axis).
  6. 6 Find the area under one arch of the cycloid $x=t-\sin t,\ y=1-\cos t$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$.
  7. 7 Find the area under the quarter-ellipse $x=3\sin t,\ y=2\cos t$, $t\in[0,\pi/2]$.
  8. 8 Find the area under $x=t^3,\ y=t^2$ for $t\in[0,1]$.
  9. 9 Find the arc length of $x=3\cos t,\ y=3\sin t$ for $t\in[0,\pi/2]$, and check it against the circumference formula.
  10. 10 Find the arc length of $x=t^2,\ y=t^3$ for $t\in[0,1]$.
  11. 11 Find the arc length of one arch of the cycloid $x=t-\sin t,\ y=1-\cos t$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$. (Hint: use $1-\cos t=2\sin^2(t/2)$.)
  12. 12 Find the arc length of the logarithmic spiral $x=e^{t}\cos t,\ y=e^{t}\sin t$ for $t\in[0,1]$.
  13. 13 Find the arc length of the astroid arc $x=\cos^3 t,\ y=\sin^3 t$ for $t\in[0,\pi/2]$. (Hint: $\sqrt{9\sin^2t\cos^2t}=3|\sin t\cos t|$.)
  14. 14 Find the arc length of $x=3t^2,\ y=2t^3$ for $t\in[0,2]$.
  15. 15 Set up (do not evaluate) the surface-area integral for revolving $x=t^2,\ y=t^3$, $t\in[0,1]$, about the $x$-axis.
  16. 16 Find the surface area generated by revolving $x=t,\ y=2\sqrt{t}$, $t\in[0,3]$, about the $x$-axis.
  17. 17 Find the surface area generated by revolving the circle $x=\cos t,\ y=\sin t$, $t\in[0,\pi]$ (the upper semicircle), about the $x$-axis, and check it against the formula for the surface area of a sphere.
  18. 18 Explain why the formula for $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ of a parametric curve is $\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)\Big/\dfrac{dx}{dt}$, and not simply $\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)$.
  19. 19 Why must the area formula $A=\int y\,dx=\int y(t)x'(t)\,dt$ use limits of $t$ that trace the curve left-to-right (increasing $x$)? What happens if you use the reverse orientation instead?
  20. 20 A wheel of radius $1$ rolls without slipping, tracing the cycloid $x=t-\sin t,\ y=1-\cos t$. Using the arc length found above, how far does a point on the rim travel during \emph{two} full rotations of the wheel ($t\in[0,4\pi]$)?

Answers

  1. 1.$\dfrac{3}{4}$
  2. 2.$-2\sqrt{2}$
  3. 3.$-\dfrac{10}{243}$
  4. 4.$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{3t^2-1}{4t^3}$; concave up where this is positive, e.g. for $t>\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$ (and on part of the negative range too — check the sign of $t^3$ as well).
  5. 5.$\dfrac{16}{3}$
  6. 6.$3\pi$
  7. 7.$\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
  8. 8.$\dfrac{3}{5}$
  9. 9.$\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ — a quarter of the circumference of a circle with radius $3$ ($2\pi(3)/4$).
  10. 10.$\dfrac{13\sqrt{13}-8}{27}\approx 1.440$
  11. 11.$8$
  12. 12.$\sqrt{2}\,(e-1)\approx 2.4923$
  13. 13.$\dfrac{3}{2}$
  14. 14.$10\sqrt{5}-2\approx 20.36$
  15. 15.$\displaystyle S=2\pi\int_0^1 t^3\sqrt{(2t)^2+(3t^2)^2}\,dt = 2\pi\int_0^1 t^4\sqrt{4+9t^2}\,dt$
  16. 16.$S=2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^3 2\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{t}}\,dt = 4\pi\int_0^3\sqrt{t+1}\,dt=\dfrac{8\pi}{3}\big(4^{3/2}-1\big)=\dfrac{56\pi}{3}\approx 58.64$
  17. 17.$S=4\pi$ — matches the full surface area of a unit sphere, $4\pi r^2$ with $r=1$.
  18. 18.$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ is itself a function of $t$, so differentiating it with respect to $x$ still requires the chain rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}=\dfrac{1}{dx/dt}\dfrac{d}{dt}$.
  19. 19.If $x$ decreases as $t$ increases, $x'(t)<0$ throughout, so the integral comes out negative — the correct area is its absolute value, or equivalently the limits of integration should be swapped.
  20. 20.$16$ (twice the length of one arch, since each rotation traces one congruent arch)
