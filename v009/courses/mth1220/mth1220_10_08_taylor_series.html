Lesson

Power series let you build a new function out of a series. Taylor series go the other direction: given a function you already know, find the power series that matches it — and all its derivatives — at a single point.

Definition — Taylor series

If $f$ has derivatives of every order at $a$, its Taylor series centered at $a$ is

$$f(x) \sim \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2 + \cdots$$

When $a=0$ this is also called a Maclaurin series. Truncating after the degree-$n$ term gives the degree-$n$ Taylor polynomial $T_n(x)$, the best polynomial approximation to $f$ near $a$ using derivative information up to order $n$.

A few series are worth memorizing outright, since building others from them (by substitution, differentiating, or integrating term by term) is usually faster than computing derivatives from the definition over and over:

Common Maclaurin series

$$e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}, \qquad \sin x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}, \qquad \cos x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$$

$$\frac{1}{1-x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \quad (|x|<1)$$

All three of the first series converge for every real $x$.

Example 1 — from the definition

Find the Maclaurin series for $f(x) = \ln(1+x)$.

Notice $f'(x) = \frac{1}{1+x}$, which is the known series above with $x$ replaced by $-x$: $\frac{1}{1+x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-x)^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n x^n$. Integrating term by term (and using $f(0)=\ln 1 = 0$ to fix the constant):

$$\ln(1+x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{n+1}}{n+1} = x - \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^3}{3} - \cdots$$

Example 2 — by substitution

Find the Maclaurin series for $f(x) = e^{-x^2}$.

Substitute $-x^2$ for $x$ directly in the known series for $e^x$:

$$e^{-x^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-x^2)^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{n!} = 1 - x^2 + \frac{x^4}{2} - \frac{x^6}{6}+\cdots$$

Far faster than differentiating $e^{-x^2}$ by hand five or six times.

  18. 18 Use the Maclaurin series for $e^x$ to evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{e^x-1-x}{x^2}$.
  19. 19 Use the Maclaurin series for $\sin(x)$ to approximate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \frac{\sin(x)}{x}\,dx$ using the first four nonzero terms of the resulting series.
  20. 20 State the binomial series for $(1+x)^k$ (general term, using the generalized binomial coefficient), and give its radius of convergence for non-integer $k$.
