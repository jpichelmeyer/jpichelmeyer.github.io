Lesson

Every test so far assumed $a_n \ge 0$. Once terms are allowed to alternate in sign, there's a new distinction to make.

Absolute vs. conditional convergence

$\sum a_n$ converges absolutely if $\sum |a_n|$ converges. If $\sum a_n$ converges but $\sum |a_n|$ does not, $\sum a_n$ converges conditionally.

Theorem: absolute convergence implies convergence. So checking $\sum |a_n|$ (an ordinary positive-term series, open to every earlier test) is always a valid way to try to establish convergence of $\sum a_n$ itself.

Alternating Series Test

Consider a series of the form $\sum (-1)^{n-1} b_n$ with $b_n > 0$. If

• $b_{n+1} \le b_n$ for all $n$ (terms are non-increasing), and

• $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} b_n = 0$,

then the series converges. Note this test only ever proves convergence, never divergence, and it says nothing about whether that convergence is absolute or conditional.

Example 1 — the alternating harmonic series

Does $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n} = 1 - \frac12 + \frac13 - \frac14 + \cdots$ converge, and if so, absolutely or conditionally?

Here $b_n = \frac1n$ is decreasing with $b_n\to 0$, so by the Alternating Series Test, the series converges. But $\sum |a_n| = \sum \frac1n$ is the harmonic series, which diverges ($p=1$). So the convergence is conditional, not absolute.

Example 2 — absolute convergence

Does $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ converge absolutely?

$\sum |a_n| = \sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ is a $p$-series with $p=2>1$, which converges. So the original series converges absolutely — and by the theorem above, that's already enough to know it converges at all, without needing the Alternating Series Test.

  16. 16 Define what it means for a series to be conditionally convergent, with an example.

  18. 18 Classify $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n\ln n}$ as absolutely convergent, conditionally convergent, or divergent.
  19. 19 Classify $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\,3}{n^{3/2}}$ as absolutely convergent, conditionally convergent, or divergent.
  20. 20 Why does the Alternating Series Test only require $b_n$ to be eventually decreasing, rather than decreasing starting at $n=1$?

Answers

  1. 1.Converges — $b_n=\tfrac1n$ is decreasing and $\to 0$.
  2. 2.Diverges — $b_n=\tfrac{n}{n+1}\to 1\ne 0$, so the terms don't go to $0$ (the $n$th-Term Test applies).
  3. 3.Converges — $b_n=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt n}$ is decreasing and $\to 0$.
  4. 4.Converges — $b_n=\tfrac{n}{n^2+1}$ is eventually decreasing and $\to 0$.
  5. 5.Diverges — $b_n=\tfrac{n!}{2^n}\to\infty\ne 0$.
  6. 6.Converges — $b_n=\tfrac{\ln n}{n}$ is eventually decreasing (for $n\ge 3$) and $\to 0$.
  7. 7.Conditionally convergent — the series itself converges (AST), but $\sum\tfrac1n$ (harmonic) diverges.
  8. 8.Absolutely convergent — $\sum\tfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges ($p=2$).
  9. 9.Absolutely convergent — $\sum\tfrac{1}{2^n}$ is a convergent geometric series.
  10. 10.Divergent — the terms don't approach $0$.
  11. 11.Partial sum $=\tfrac58=0.625$; error $\le b_5=\tfrac{1}{5!}=\tfrac1{120}\approx 0.0083$.
  12. 12.Need $b_{n+1}=\tfrac{1}{(n+1)^3}<0.001$, i.e. $(n+1)^3>1000$, so $n+1\ge 11$: use $n=10$ terms.
  13. 13.Partial sum $=\tfrac{19}{30}\approx 0.6333$; error $\le b_6=\tfrac{1}{6!}=\tfrac1{720}\approx 0.0014$.
  14. 14.Need $b_{n+1}=\tfrac{1}{2(n+1)+1}<0.01$, i.e. $2n+3>100$, so $n\ge 49$: use $n=49$ terms.
  15. 15.$\sum a_n$ is absolutely convergent if $\sum|a_n|$ converges. Every absolutely convergent series is also (ordinarily) convergent — absolute convergence is the stronger condition.
  16. 16.A series that converges, but not absolutely — i.e. $\sum a_n$ converges while $\sum|a_n|$ diverges. Example: $\sum \tfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$.
  17. 17.A conditionally convergent series can be rearranged (reordered) to converge to any real number, or to diverge — absolutely convergent series always converge to the same sum regardless of rearrangement.
  18. 18.Conditionally convergent — the Alternating Series Test applies ($b_n=\tfrac{1}{n\ln n}\to 0$, decreasing), but $\sum\tfrac{1}{n\ln n}$ diverges (by the Integral Test).
  19. 19.Absolutely convergent — $\sum\tfrac{3}{n^{3/2}}$ converges ($p=\tfrac32>1$).
  20. 20.Convergence of a series depends only on the behavior of its tail — changing or ignoring finitely many terms at the start never affects whether the series converges, so it's enough for the decrease to kick in eventually.
