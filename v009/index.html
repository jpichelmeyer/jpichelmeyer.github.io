After starting undergrad as an art major, I somehow ended up with a PhD in mathematics and a second masters degree in computer science. Some people like to really dig into a single area (which can be very useful), but I personally find that far too limiting.

For me, the most fun part of research isn’t sticking to a single path—it’s seeing how totally different ideas fit together. I tend to follow interesting concepts wherever they take me, which has driven me to explore a diverse array of topics such as knot theory, neuroscience, game design, and now artificial life. I’m just endlessly curious about how complex, clever behavior emerges out of simple parts, whether in code, brains, or nature.

In both my teaching and my work, I try to keep things grounded and fun. I don't care much for rigid academic borders; I’d rather hunt for surprising connections and just follow the coolest questions wherever they lead.
